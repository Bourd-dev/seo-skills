# Comparison pages

Use this when shaping or reviewing comparison, alternative, "versus", or ranked-list review pages.

## Core stance

Treat strong comparison pages as review-grade decision support, not sales theater.

- Google explicitly includes head-to-head comparisons and ranked recommendations within review content.
- The quality bar is original research, real expertise, useful analysis, and evidence that helps someone choose.
- A page should exist because it helps a real evaluator make a better decision, not merely because a query variant exists.

## What deserves a page

A comparison page is justified when all four conditions hold:

1. There is credible evaluation demand or business value.
2. The page can answer a real decision a buyer is trying to make.
3. The team has enough truthful evidence to say something materially better than a generic feature grid.
4. The site's larger SEO foundation is healthy enough that expansion is not a distraction from more important blockers.

## Useful page formats

- **Single alternative**: "best alternative to X" when the reader is considering a switch.
- **Ranked alternatives list**: a shortlist when the reader is still surveying options.
- **Head-to-head**: "A vs B" when the comparison itself is the task.
- **Scenario-led comparison**: "best for [use case]" when the important choice depends on circumstance more than brand.

Use the fewest formats that satisfy real demand. Do not create a combinatorial page set merely because the nouns can be permuted.

## Evidence standard

Prefer:

- first-hand product use, screenshots, recordings, or tested workflows
- quantitative measurements where they help the decision
- current pricing, packaging, and capability checks with dates
- customer or operator experience that reveals switching costs, edge cases, or trade-offs
- visible expertise from the author or reviewer
- citations or links to primary sources for claims that cannot be verified directly

Track for every material claim:

- claim
- source
- freshness date
- confidence
- what would invalidate it

## Page-quality rules

- Start from the user's decision, not the company's preferred positioning.
- Define the key decision factors before drafting the comparison.
- Explain meaningful differences, not just feature presence or absence.
- State who each option is best for and who it is not best for.
- Discuss benefits and drawbacks from evidence, not insinuation.
- Explain important design choices and how they affect users.
- Include enough useful analysis that the page stands on its own.
- Prefer one strong page over several thin near-duplicates.

## Common failure modes

- feature tables copied from vendor websites
- unsupported negatives about compared products, services, or brands
- fake neutrality that hides the commercial point of view
- interchangeable intros with one brand token swapped
- pages written before the team has used the compared products
- stale pricing or capability claims with no owner for refresh
- page proliferation that outruns evidence or search demand
