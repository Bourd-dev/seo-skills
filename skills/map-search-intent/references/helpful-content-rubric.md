# Helpful-content rubric

Use this when a technical audit needs to decide whether an accessible page is also worth recommending for visibility.

## Questions to answer

- Does the page contribute original information, analysis, experience, or synthesis beyond what is already common across the web?
- Is the page's purpose clear, and does it achieve that purpose well for people?
- Is the treatment substantial enough for the user's likely task?
- Is the source of expertise legible through authorship, evidence, background, or firsthand detail?
- Are claims accurate and supported?
- If the topic is trust-sensitive, is the page's sourcing, expertise, and confidence level strong enough for the stakes?
- Would the page feel satisfying to a real visitor who arrived with this need?
- Is the page primarily for people, or primarily to capture traffic?

## Risk signals

- Thin rewrites of already-common advice
- Many near-duplicate pages created only to target query variants
- Unsupported factual claims
- Hidden or weak authorship where expertise matters
- AI-assisted content published at scale without visible added value
- pages whose apparent purpose is to capture traffic rather than help people

## Reporting guidance

- Do not reduce quality to word count.
- Quote or summarize concrete examples from the page.
- Separate low distinctiveness from factual unreliability; they are different problems.
