# Audit helpful content

## Goal

Decide whether accessible pages are also worth recommending to real users.

## Workflow

1. Read `references/helpful-content-rubric.md`, `references/ai-search-guidance.md`, and `references/audit-output-contract.md`.
2. Select representative pages by business importance, template, traffic opportunity, and content age.
3. Review for:
   - original information, analysis, or firsthand experience
   - completeness relative to the user's likely task
   - visible expertise and trust signals
   - factual accuracy and sourcing
   - obvious commodity-content patterns
   - evidence of scaled low-value generation
4. Compare pages against likely alternatives in the result set when that evidence is available.
5. Separate three judgments:
   - technically accessible
   - adequate but ordinary
   - genuinely distinctive and satisfying
6. Escalate factual unreliability separately from low distinctiveness.

## Decision rules

- Do not reduce quality to word count.
- Treat AI assistance as neutral; judge the finished page.
- Prefer concrete page evidence over generic editorial advice.

## Output

Produce:

- a content-quality summary by sampled page or template
- recurring trust and distinctiveness gaps
- prioritized findings using the shared audit contract

## Before returning output

Run these checks against the draft before handing it back.

1. Quality judgments cite specific page evidence: a missing source, a stale date, a copied paragraph, a thin section. Generic "this could be improved" is dropped.
2. AI-assistance is not the finding. The finding is the manipulative pattern, the inaccuracy, or the missing first-hand experience visible in the finished page.
3. Factual unreliability is escalated as its own category, separate from low distinctiveness. A wrong page and a forgettable page are different problems.
4. Verbs name their object. A page is not "weak" or "underperforming" without naming the dimension (originality, completeness, trust, freshness) and the evidence behind the call.
5. No em-dashes. No "X, not Y" reversals. No filler ("delve," "landscape," "navigate" as metaphor, "journey," "underscore").
