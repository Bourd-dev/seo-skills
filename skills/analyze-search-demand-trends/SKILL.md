# Analyze search demand trends

## Goal

Turn Google Trends data into grounded demand insights without mistaking trendiness for strategy.

## Workflow

1. Read `references/search-demand-trends.md`, `references/search-opportunities.md`, and `references/helpful-content-rubric.md`.
2. Establish the analysis purpose:
   - rising trends
   - keyword research
   - content timing
   - regional comparison
   - industry benchmarking
3. Use Trends evidence where relevant:
   - interest over time
   - top and rising topics or queries
   - related topics and queries
   - regional interest
   - comparisons across markets, categories, or properties
4. Distinguish:
   - sustained demand
   - rising demand
   - seasonal demand
   - localized demand
   - industry-wide movement
5. For each promising signal, check:
   - fit with the site's business
   - fit with the audience
   - whether the site has first-hand experience or expertise
   - whether the current content set already addresses it well
6. Hand justified opportunities to:
   - `map-search-opportunities` for broader opportunity synthesis
   - `diagnose-search-traffic-drop` when the question is whether demand, rather than site quality, explains a change

## Decision rules

- Do not recommend content just because a topic is trending.
- Do not compare markets as if their seasonality is interchangeable.
- Prefer topic interpretation over brittle single-query conclusions when Trends data is aggregated at the topic level.

## Output

Produce:

- demand-trend summary
- rising, seasonal, regional, and industry signals
- candidate opportunities with fit assessment
- timing or localization implications when supported

## Before returning output

Run these checks against the draft before handing it back.

1. Trendiness on its own is never the justification. Every candidate opportunity carries a fit check: business, audience, and existing coverage.
2. Topic-level and query-level evidence are labelled separately. Trends data aggregated at the topic level is not used to support claims about a single phrasing.
3. Regional and seasonal patterns are scoped to the markets they were observed in. Patterns are not extrapolated across markets without separate evidence.
4. Verbs name their object. A topic is not "growing" or "declining" without saying over what window, in what market, and by which Trends measure.
5. No em-dashes. No "X, not Y" reversals. No filler ("delve," "landscape," "navigate" as metaphor, "journey," "underscore").
